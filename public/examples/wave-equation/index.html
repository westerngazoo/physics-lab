1 · One bead, Newton's law

Pick the highlighted bead. It has mass m = μ·Δx — its share of a string with mass per length μ. Newton says its transverse acceleration obeys:

m · yᵢ̇̇ = Fᵢ

Everything that follows is bookkeeping on Fᵢ.

2 · The force comes from its two neighbours

The only things touching the bead are the two string segments (gold). Each pulls with tension T along itself; for small slopes the transverse component of each pull is T times the segment's slope. The net transverse force — the red arrow on the stage — is the difference of the two slopes:

Fᵢ = T · [ (yᵢ₊₁ − yᵢ)/Δx − (yᵢ − yᵢ₋₁)/Δx ]

A bead on a straight stretch feels nothing: the two pulls cancel. Only where the string bends is there a net force — watch the arrow vanish wherever the chain is locally straight.

3 · The bracket is curvature

Collect the bracket:

yᵢ̇̇ = (T/μ) · (yᵢ₊₁ − 2yᵢ + yᵢ₋₁) / Δx²

That second difference is exactly how you estimate a second derivative from three samples — it is the string's curvature, sampled at bead spacing. The force law says: acceleration is proportional to curvature. Peaks accelerate down, valleys accelerate up, straight parts coast.

4 · Send Δx → 0

Hold μ and T fixed and pack in more beads. The second difference over Δx² becomes the second derivative, and Newton's law on every bead becomes one statement about a smooth field:

∂²y/∂t² = c² · ∂²y/∂x²,   c = √(T/μ)

Now do it. Drag the beads slider up and watch the pluck speed vs c readout climb toward 100% — that number is the limit being taken. The wave equation is not an extra law of nature; it is Newton's law with the bookkeeping finished.

Why the motion is exact. Nothing here is stepped forward in time. The chain's vibration modes are known in closed form, so the pluck is decomposed once into modes and every later shape is a direct function of t — verified against an independent step-by-step integration to 2×10⁻⁹ before this page was built. Pause, scrub, replay: nothing can drift.

Watch d'Alembert happen. The pluck immediately splits into two half-height pulses travelling opposite ways at speed c — the travelling-wave solution y = f(x−ct) + g(x+ct) in the flesh. They pass through each other unchanged (superposition: displacements add, and where they disagree they cancel — destructive interference), and flip upside down at each wall, the fixed-end reflection.

The honest wrinkle. The discrete chain is dispersive: its very shortest waves — beads alternating up and down — travel at only 2/π ≈ 64% of c, and no bead count fixes that. What raising N fixes is every wavelength a smooth pluck actually contains. The pluck speed vs c readout is therefore weighted by the pluck's own energy spectrum: that is the number that goes to 100%, and that is the precise sense in which the wave equation is the limit of the chain.
