Watch the doubling. Sweep mirror B and compare the two readouts: the rotation of v is always exactly twice the mirror angle. Drag the v slider instead and the rotation does not change at all — the rotor R = b a never heard of v. That is why rotors compose cleanly where angle bookkeeping gets messy: they are built from the mirrors, not from the things being rotated.

The gold × is the whole argument. The red arrow is computed the long way: two sandwiches, b (a v a) b. The × is one sandwich with the rotor R = b a. They coincide to the last floating-point digit — the two paths differ by readout shows the actual separation, around 10⁻¹⁶ — because they are the same expression with the parentheses moved. Associativity is the proof.

Try the degenerate cases. Set both mirrors to the same angle: the rotation reads 0 — reflecting twice across one mirror undoes itself. Set them a quarter turn (0.25 τ) apart: v comes back rotated half a turn, and the rotor reads ⟨R⟩₀ = 0, pure e₁₂.

Verify it yourself. The claims on this page are assertions in the same crate the page runs: cargo test -p lesson-two-mirrors checks C1–C5 across a τ-fraction grid of mirror and vector angles — including that the rotor's components follow cos Δ and −sin Δ, pinned against garust's own conventions rather than assumed.
