The knobs. g is gravity — the strength of the constant pull, Moon (1.62) to Jupiter (24.79). Restitution e is the fraction of speed the ball keeps at each impact: leave the floor at e times the arrival speed. Since kinetic energy goes as speed squared, each bounce keeps e² of the energy — that is exactly the staircase in panel III, and the rest leaves as sound, heat and deformation, which is the thud you would hear. ε bends the flight path away from the true one (panel I, dashed) so Lagrange has something to score. The time slider runs in fractions of the total time-to-rest, so one sweep spans any gravity.

I · Newton. One statement: the force is constant, −g per unit mass, drawn as the red arrow on the ball — the same length at the apex as at the floor, which is the whole content of the law. Slide gravity and the arrow grows while every clock tightens; the apex heights never move, because hₙ = e²ₙh₀ contains no g.

II · Lagrange. Forget forces. Consider every path the ball could take between two bounces, and score each by its action S = ∫(T − V)dt. Drag the ε slider to bend the path (dashed, panel I) and watch its score climb the parabola in panel II: the flown path sits exactly at the bottom, and the excess is exactly ε²K — the linear term vanishes because the flown path obeys Newton. Stationary action and F = ma are one claim wearing two notations.

III · Hamilton. Forget paths. The whole state is one point (y, p), and it rides a level set of H = p²/2 + g y — the nested curves in panel III, one per flight. In flight, H is conserved to the last digit. At each bounce the point teleports down to the next level set: H → e²H, every time, exactly. That staircase is where this system stops being conservative — and seeing where a formulation's guarantee ends is as much the lesson as the guarantee.
